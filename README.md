# Whole Flat Audio

One Spotify speaker for the whole flat: an Android TV app that plays Spotify in sync in the living room and the kitchen.

**What it is:** an app for the Chromecast with Google TV that shows up in Spotify as a single speaker, "Whole Flat". It plays the same music in the living room and on the kitchen Sonos at the same time, in sync, with no extra hardware.

**How it works**
- **Spotify → Google TV:** Spotify Connect at the highest quality (320 kbps), via librespot. You pick "Whole Flat" in the Spotify app on your phone.
- **Living room:** Google TV → HDMI → Philips TV, with the screen switched off over the network (JointSpace) → optical → amplifier → speakers.
- **Kitchen:** the app streams the same audio losslessly (FLAC) to a Sonos speaker over the home network (UPnP).
- **Sync:** both rooms start together. The app tracks the Sonos's playback position and corrects clock drift continuously, with a target of ±10 ms between the rooms.

**Features**
- Pause, skip and seek act in both rooms at once.
- The kitchen joins by itself when the Sonos wakes up or returns from Bluetooth, and the living room never stops.
- The living room stays at full volume, set on the amp, and the phone volume controls the kitchen. This is a setting.
- When music starts, the TV is woken if it is in standby (best effort) and turns its screen off, but not while you're watching video.
- It starts with the Google TV, reconnects to Spotify by itself and recovers after sleep.
- **A second way in:** when Spotify refuses the "Whole Flat" device, cast to the Chromecast itself ("Den TV") instead, and the kitchen follows a copy of what it plays (see "If Spotify refuses Whole Flat" below).
- **Synced mode:** the living room is played through the TV's own DLNA player as a live lyrics video (H.264 and AAC in a constant-rate MPEG transport stream, with the lyrics from LRCLIB), and the kitchen is held to the same delay, so both rooms are in step with the lyrics on the screen (see "Synced mode" below). A phone page picks what the TV shows: Listening, Ambilight, or Screen + Ambilight.
- **Remote-friendly settings:** kitchen on/off, sync offset, kitchen balance and TV options.
- **Status page and calibration:** a status page in the browser, and a 30-second calibration from the doorway using a Mac (or by ear, with the Test button on the TV).

**Requirements:** a Chromecast with Google TV (or another device with Android TV 8 or newer), Spotify Premium, a Sonos speaker on the same network with UPnP on, and optionally a Philips TV with JointSpace. It's installed via the Downloader app from the GitHub release link.

Spotify Premium is really needed: librespot calls `exit(1)` for a non-Premium account, which ends the app process.

## Install

### With the Downloader app (no computer)

1. On the TV: Settings, System, About, click "Android TV OS build" seven times to enable developer options.
2. Install **Downloader** from the Play Store and allow it to install unknown apps (Settings, Apps, Security & restrictions, Unknown sources).
3. Open Downloader and enter `https://github.com/matijasalej/flat_audio/releases/latest/download/WholeFlatAudio.apk`, then install. The same address installs updates (the app is signed with the same key every time).

### With adb

```sh
adb pair <tv-ip>:<pairing-port>     # Settings, System, Developer options, Wireless debugging
adb connect <tv-ip>:<port>
adb install -r WholeFlatAudio.apk
```

**Wireless debugging switches itself off after every reboot** of the Google TV. Switch it on again in Settings, System, Developer options, Wireless debugging (with the remote), then look up the new port from the Mac: `adb mdns services` lists `_adb-tls-connect._tcp` with the address and port, and `adb connect <tv-ip>:<port>` connects (the port changes each time; an already paired Mac does not need the pairing code again).

## First-time setup

1. Open **Whole Flat Audio** once on the TV and press **Start service**. From then on it starts by itself at boot. (Allow the notification permission if the TV asks.)
2. In the Spotify app on your phone, open the device list and pick **Whole Flat** once. No typing: the phone hands over the login through zeroconf, and the app keeps it for later boots.
3. **Sonos:** make sure UPnP is on for the speaker, and that its battery saver or auto-sleep does not take it off the network too early. If it does go to sleep, the kitchen joins again by itself when it wakes up. Something that is *paused* on the Sonos does not keep the kitchen out (see "Who has the Sonos" below); something that plays does.
4. **Philips TV:** turn off the TV's switch-off timer, or it will turn itself off while music plays. JointSpace (port 1925) may only open after the TV has been unplugged from the mains for a minute once. The app finds the TV by itself; if it does not, type its address on the settings screen.
5. **Google TV:** the energy saver (Settings, System, Power & energy) **must be Never**. A Google TV that goes to sleep freezes the audio output (the app notices and rebuilds it, but it cannot play while the box sleeps). Also switch off "turn off the device with the TV" in the HDMI-CEC settings. The screen timeout of the box (`screen_off_timeout`, 10 min here) is a sleep of its own once the screensaver is off: a Cast session of Spotify does not keep the box awake, and the box's sleep sends CEC Standby to the TV, which switched the TV and the music off ten minutes after the cast began. So while the music plays (the source is not idle) the app holds a screen wake lock (`wholeflat:music`, `box_awake` in `/status.json`); when the music stops it lets go and the timeout starts again, so the box and the TV still go off ten minutes after the music.
6. **Grants over adb**, once (they survive updates and reboots):

   ```sh
   adb shell appops set io.github.matijasalej.wholeflat GET_USAGE_STATS allow
   ```

   This lets the app see which app is in front, so that the TV's screen is not switched off under a video. Without it the screen is left on.

   The four grants of the capture mode are in "If Spotify refuses Whole Flat" below; they are only needed if you play to the Chromecast itself.
7. **Calibrate** the two rooms (below) once, and again if the speakers or the TV's audio delay change.

## One player per Spotify account

Only one librespot-based player should run per Spotify account at a time. This app is one; the Spotify app on a computer with librespot, Raspberry Pi players, spotifyd and similar are others. Every player asks Spotify for an audio key for every track it loads, and a few players on one account mean a few times the requests, which is how an account ends up with its keys refused (see below). Switch the others off while this one is in use.

## If Spotify refuses playback

Some accounts get the audio keys of their tracks refused by Spotify for a while (librespot issue #1649). It is a refusal on Spotify's side that is tied to the account, not a fault of the app: a stock librespot on a computer gets the same. The usual librespot answer, skipping to the next track, makes it worse, because each skipped track asks for a key again.

This app does not skip. When a key is refused, it:

- **pauses** (the Spotify app on the phone shows the device as paused) and **keeps the same track**,
- **tries that track again by itself** after 3 s, 10 s, 30 s, 2 min, 5 min and then every 15 min,
- never sends two key requests less than 3 s apart after an error,
- **gives up after about 30 minutes** of refusals and waits for you.

Your own commands are respected: **play** in the Spotify app tries again at once (but not sooner than 3 s after the last refusal) and starts the back-off over, **pause** stops the tries until you press play, and loading another track or position starts over from the beginning.

What you see meanwhile:

- on the TV, the first lines of the status say *Spotify is refusing playback for this account right now, retrying in 12 s*, or, after the 30 minutes, that it kept refusing and that you should press play on the phone;
- in `/status.json`: `"spotify_state": "key_refused"` (or `"key_gave_up"`, `"ok"`), `"retry_in_s"` and `"count"` (the refusals of this episode), and the object `spotify_key` with the phase (`waiting`, `retrying`, `user_paused`, `gave_up`) and the total since the app started;
- in the log (`/log`): lines tagged `spotify` and `native` for every refusal, try and recovery.

If it does not clear up, wait. The refusals have gone away by themselves after some hours; do not keep pressing play (the app already spaces its requests, and every press starts the back-off over).

## If Spotify refuses Whole Flat: play to Den TV

Spotify can refuse the audio keys of an account for a long time (see above), and then nothing helps but a player that is not librespot. The Chromecast is one: in the Spotify app on the phone pick **the Chromecast itself** (here "Den TV") instead of "Whole Flat". The Chromecast plays the music by itself, to the living room, through the TV and the amplifier as always. This app then **captures that audio** (Android's AudioPlaybackCapture of the Cast receiver, `com.google.android.apps.mediashell`, and of the Spotify TV app, `com.spotify.tv.android`) and sends a copy to the Sonos. It is the same kitchen, fed from another source.

**Four grants over adb, once** (they survive updates and reboots; without them the capture waits, and the status screen says which one is missing):

```sh
adb shell pm grant io.github.matijasalej.wholeflat android.permission.RECORD_AUDIO
adb shell appops set io.github.matijasalej.wholeflat PROJECT_MEDIA allow
adb shell appops set io.github.matijasalej.wholeflat SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW allow
adb shell cmd notification allow_listener io.github.matijasalej.wholeflat/io.github.matijasalej.wholeflat.capture.MediaWatcher
```

A fifth grant is optional: `adb shell pm grant io.github.matijasalej.wholeflat android.permission.WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS` lets the app switch the screensaver off while the system runs (see "Keeping the box light" below); without it the screensaver is left as it is and `/sys` says so.

`RECORD_AUDIO` is the permission Android asks for any capture of audio, `PROJECT_MEDIA` lets the app have its screen capture permission without the dialog on the TV, `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` lets it show the (invisible) permission screen from the background, for example after a reboot, and the notification listener access lets it read the play/pause state and the title of the two players, so that the kitchen can be made ready a moment before the sound. The last one is optional: without it the kitchen starts about 0.2 s later, on the sound itself. Without adb, the app asks for the recording permission when it opens, and the button **Capture: ask for permission now** makes the TV show the screen capture dialog (answer it with the remote); but that dialog then comes back every time the capture starts, for example after a reboot, which is what the app op avoids.

**What it does, by itself:** the capture starts with the app (also after a boot) and is shown on the status screen (`Capture: running, level -23 dBFS`). Sound for 200 ms is a start: the kitchen is prepared at the first sound and Play goes out at once. Silence for 2 s is a pause, but the kitchen **keeps streaming** (silence) for up to a minute, so skipping a track or pausing for a bit never restarts it; after a minute of silence the Sonos is muted and stopped, and the next sound starts it again. If the projection is lost the capture starts again with a wait of 2, 5, 15, 30 and then 60 s.

**Which source wins:** if the Spotify app plays on "Whole Flat" (librespot) and the Chromecast plays at the same time, librespot wins and the capture is ignored; when librespot stops, the capture takes over if its player still plays. `source` in `/status.json` and on the screen says `librespot`, `capture` or `idle`.

**The kitchen is late, by design, but not by much.** A capture has no second room to be in step with: the Chromecast plays the living room by itself, at once, and the copy can only reach the Sonos after it was captured, through the Sonos's own buffer. The lag of the kitchen (`kitchen.lag_ms`, the time from a frame being captured to the Sonos playing it) is made of three parts: the **capture pipeline** (how old the newest frame is when it arrives here, `capture.pipeline_ms`, from the timestamps of `AudioRecord`; the reads are 10 ms, and a WAV stream carries each at once), the **lead** (audio sent ahead of "now" at the start, `capture_lead_ms`, from 10 ms) and the Sonos's **Play-to-sound delay** (about 0.19 s for WAV, which captured streams use, 0.29 s for FLAC). The app measures the lag in the first seconds of a stream and then either holds it where it locked (the default) or brings it to a target.

**Bringing the lag to a target.** The lag is brought to `kitchen_target_ms` (322 ms by default: measured on the device with WAV, the Sonos is clean down to a lag of 316 ms, stutters at 306, and a fresh start followed by one descent to 322 is clean). After the lag has locked, the controller moves it to the target gradually, with the same frame trims that hold it: to lower the lag the stream drops a frame, so the Sonos's buffer shrinks; to raise it a frame is repeated. That is at most 3 ms of lag per second, a speed change of 0.3 % that cannot be heard (`kitchen.setpoint_ms` is where the controller holds the lag at that moment, `kitchen.steering` says `descending`, `ascending` or `holding`, and `kitchen.descending` is true while the lag is on its way down). The lag is never aimed below the **floor**: the setting `kitchen_floor_ms` (316 ms), or what an underrun showed if that is higher. A Sonos that is driven below what it can keep up with runs out of audio, and the controller hides it (it drops frames to keep the lag where it was told, however often the Sonos pauses), so the signs are: it reports buffering (websocket) or TRANSITIONING, the websocket reports a playback error, the lag readings jump up or scatter, or the position it reports advances slower than real time (a robust fit over about 2 s; `kitchen.position_behind_ms_per_s`, a stutter you can hear shows here before anything else). Any of them, while the lag is below where the steering began, **starts the stream again** (a fresh start; raising the setpoint of a Sonos that has stuttered does not stop it) with a **learned floor** 15 ms above the lag where it happened. The learned floor is kept for the rest of the local day, also across restarts of the app (`kitchen.floor_learned_ms`, `floor_day`, logged as "the speaker ran out of audio"), and forgotten at midnight or when `kitchen_floor_ms`, `kitchen_format` or the speaker changes; `kitchen.floor_ms` is the floor in force. A lag step that is no such sign (the Sonos stalled once at the lag it chose itself) is measured again instead of restarting the stream. `kitchen.room_offset_ms` is the lag less the target, so it is near 0 once the kitchen has arrived, and `kitchen.target_ms` is the target in use (null: none).

| Setting | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `capture_lead_ms` (10 to 3000, default 80) | How much audio is sent to the Sonos ahead of the newest captured frame when the stream starts (`kitchen.lead_ms` is what it was at Play). The Sonos starts when it has about 270 ms buffered whatever the lead (a smaller lead only makes it start later), so the lag at the start is about 350 ms; 80 ms is what ran best. It is read when a stream starts. If the Sonos turns out to need more than asked while no target is steered to, the app remembers that until it is restarted or you change this setting, and starts the next streams that far ahead; `kitchen.capture_lead_ms` is the lead in use and `kitchen.capture_lead_learned_ms` what was learned. |
| `kitchen_target_ms` (-1 or 0 to 3000, default 322) | The lag to bring the kitchen to, in ms after the capture. Absolute: it applies to every stream. -1 (or `off`) leaves the lag where it locked. The target is never below `kitchen_floor_ms` or the learned floor. |
| `kitchen_floor_ms` (100 to 3000, default 316) | The lowest lag that is aimed for. |
| `kitchen_offset_ms` | No effect in capture mode: the start of a stream is its lead, and the lag is its target (a persistent negative offset used to make every start later). It still works for Spotify's own playback (Whole Flat). |
| `capture_living_latency_ms` (default 0) | How much later than it is captured the living room sounds (the Chromecast's output buffer and the TV's delay). When `kitchen_target_ms` is off (-1) and this is above 0, it is the target. |
| `kitchen_volume` (0 to 100, -1 = leave the Sonos alone, default -1) | The volume of the Sonos for captured music. The slider of the phone page sets it; it is applied at the start of every stream and at once when it changes. |
| `capture_volume_mirror` (default off) | Makes the Sonos follow the music volume of this box (the phone's Cast volume) on a box where Cast changes it. Off here. |
| `kitchen_format` (`auto`, `flac`, `wav`) | The stream to the Sonos. `auto` (the default) is WAV for captured music and FLAC for Spotify's; a `flac` stored by an older version is reset to `auto` once. |
| `capture_enabled` (default on) | Switches the capture off. |

Set them with `curl 'http://<tv-ip>:8080/settings?capture_lead_ms=300'`.

**Volume.** On this setup the phone's Cast volume ("Den TV" in the Spotify app) changes nothing, and that is as intended: the HDMI link is a full-volume device with HDMI-CEC volume control switched off, because the living room is turned on the amplifier with the Google TV remote (infrared to the Onkyo), and enabling CEC volume control could break that remote. The app never reads or changes the box's music volume and never touches HDMI or CEC settings. The **kitchen** is the only thing the phone controls: the slider and the plus and minus buttons of the phone page (`kitchen_volume`, the Sonos volume, kept in the settings and applied at the start of every stream; the Sonos app keeps working too). On a box where Cast does change the music volume, `capture_volume_mirror` makes the Sonos follow it (the old behaviour, including the fixed living-room volume that puts the box back to its maximum after each change); it is off by default and does nothing unless it is on. Spotify's own playback (Whole Flat) is different: there the volume of the phone is real, and the Sonos follows it with the kitchen balance.

**Keep the Spotify TV app as it is.** A new version of the Spotify app can opt out of being captured (or play through another path), and then the capture hears silence. Once it works, switch off automatic updates for it: in the Play Store on the TV open the Spotify page, and in its menu switch off *Enable auto update* (or switch off auto-update for all apps in the Play Store's settings). The Cast receiver is part of the system and changes only with a system update.

**Not done in this mode:** the TV's picture is not switched off and the TV is not woken (the Chromecast shows its own screen for the cast), and the living room is never touched: our own audio output only plays silence and never the captured music. Anything else that the Cast receiver plays is captured as well, for example a video cast from the phone, and also goes to the kitchen.

## Synced mode: both rooms through the TV's own player

In the capture mode above the living room is played by the Chromecast itself, at once, and the kitchen can only follow a little later. **Synced mode** turns that round: the box hands the TV a live video of its own, through the TV's DLNA player (the Philips renderer at `http://<tv-ip>:2870/dmr.xml`), and holds the kitchen to the same delay, so that the sound of the two rooms and the lyrics on the screen are one thing. The delay is long (about 2.4 s: the TV buffers what it is given), which only matters when you press pause or skip. It is the default mode (`mode`, `synced`); `lyrics` is the other, see below.

**What the TV shows** is the setting `display`, chosen on the phone page (`http://<box-ip>:8080/remote`) or with the button at the top of the app on the TV; all three are Synced mode, the stream is never restarted by a change of it (it is only the screen and the Ambilight of the TV, through JointSpace), and it goes back to Listening after ten minutes without music:

| `display` | Screen | Ambilight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `listen` (default) | off | off |
| `ambilight` | off | on, following the audio (`FOLLOW_AUDIO`, `ENERGY_ADAPTIVE_BRIGHTNESS`; what it was before is put back afterwards) |
| `screen` | the lyrics video | on, the same style |

The phone page is these three buttons and the volume of the kitchen (the slider and the plus and minus buttons, `kitchen_volume`), and nothing else. It is a page for the home screen of an iPhone (Safari, Share, Add to Home Screen: a standalone icon, `manifest.webmanifest`, `apple-touch-icon`); on plain HTTP Android's Chrome makes only a shortcut of it. Give the box a fixed address in the router so that the link stays. The fine tuning is on the debug page (`http://<box-ip>:8080`, at the top): the extra of the TV (`tv_extra_ms`), the fine sync of the kitchen against the TV (`kitchen_offset_ms`, 10 ms a press), the lyrics against the sound (`lyrics_offset_ms`), and the tools described below. **Lyrics mode** (the Spotify app on the screen over HDMI with its own lyrics, the kitchen at 322 ms) is not on the phone page: it is entered when the TV's own remote leaves the player (Home, or the Spotify key), the Ambilight there is the display's (on for Ambilight and Screen + Ambilight while the music plays, off for Listening), and a choice of a display on the phone or the TV app goes back to Synced. A Cast session that takes the TV (the Chromecast's own screen) is waited out and the TV taken again after 3 s; another video app in front means the TV is let go and not taken again while it is in front.

**How a grab goes.** The mode manager wakes the TV if it sleeps, reads `dmr.xml`, and has the TV play `http://<box-ip>:8080/k/<token>.ts` (Stop, SetAVTransportURI with a `videoItem`, Play). The TV opens about four connections in its first seconds, three short ones to look at the stream and a last that it stays on, 2.5 to 4.2 s after Play, and what it plays hitches meanwhile. A TV that was stopped a moment ago (a re-anchor) says 705 (the transport is locked) or 701 to the item until it has settled: the grab waits for its transport to say stopped and tries SetAVTransportURI again every 1.2 s for about 14 s, each try in the log; a re-anchor sends one Stop, not two. While a stream is being made again, and for 30 s after a start failed, the kitchen keeps its delay of Synced mode (it is not sent to the Lyrics mode's lag and back) and the TV is tried again even if the music pauses meanwhile. So every grab has a **warm-up**: the stream carries a splash ("Whole Flat" on the colour of the cover) and silence until the TV plays, has one connection open and has not asked for another for 2.5 s (25 s at the most); then the audio switches to the music at a block boundary and the picture to the lyrics (or black) at an IDR picture, and the kitchen, which was held back meanwhile (a kitchen that already plays is not touched), is started or started again so that its first sound is the TV's first sound. `warmup_ms` and why it ended are in `/tv/dlna` and the log. Changing songs never does this.

**The stream.** The audio is captured, encoded to AAC (`c2.android.aac.encoder`), the lyrics picture (1920 x 1080, 30 a second) is drawn on the input surface of the hardware H.264 encoder (`c2.amlogic.avc.encoder`, High profile, 6 Mbit/s constant bitrate, an IDR every second, no B-frames), and both go into a **constant-rate transport stream**: a packet every 167 us (9 Mbit/s), null packets where nothing is sent, the PCR exactly what the position in the stream says, the tables every 100 ms and before every IDR, audio before video so that a 130 ms key picture never holds the sound up. In the device tests the TV held its latency to 1 ms through every change of song with such a stream, and with the stream as the encoder makes it (bursts at the key pictures) its latency came out different for every song, between 0.2 and 1.2 s. The picture is the design of the mock: the colour of the cover behind big bold lines (Figtree, SIL Open Font License, in `assets/fonts/`) that scroll as the song goes, the line that is sung in white, the footer with the cover, the title, the artist and a progress bar; without lyrics the cover, the title and the artist in the middle with "No lyrics for this song". The lyrics come from LRCLIB (exact lookup, without the album, with the title cleaned of "feat." and "remastered", first artist, then the search; kept, and not asked for again for an hour if there are none); the line that is lit is the one of the position that the sound of that moment has, so the picture and the sound of the TV agree (`lyrics_offset_ms` moves it). `/tv/preview.jpg` shows what the picture shows now.

**The timing.** A frame is written to the TV one **hold** after it was captured (the pipeline, the encoders and a margin, about 210 ms; fixed for the life of the stream, because the TV follows the PTS and PCR it was given). A connection starts at the newest key picture that is at least `tv_burst_ms` (1500) old (or at the oldest one held, when the stream is younger than that: so Play is only sent when the stream holds the burst, a second or less after it began), gets everything from there to the live edge at once and then the stream as it is made. The TV plays from that key picture at the pace it is given the data, so its latency from our write to its sound is **the age of that key picture when the connection was made (the backlog) plus `tv_extra_ms` (631)**, the backlog not counted as less than `tv_preroll_ms` (1050): against the doorway recording 1.60 s gave 2.23 s, and 0.79 s gave 1.68 s. The backlog of the newest open connection is what counts, and it is worked out again whenever the TV makes or drops a connection (`backlog_ms`, `delay_ms` in `/tv/dlna`). The **common delay D** is the hold plus that, and the kitchen is **set once** at D (plus `kitchen_offset_ms`, the fine sync) and then left alone: its stream starts with a lead of about 2 s so that the Sonos has the audio long before it plays it, and when it has landed (the Sonos starts 100 ms or so later with such a lead than it plans for; the app learns how much, `plan_bias_ms` in `/kitchen`) it is trimmed once to D at 0.45 % of the speed, or started again if it is more than 250 ms off. After that nothing is trimmed: the Sonos and the TV run on clocks that agree to about 20 ppm, and a controller that follows the readings of a stream with a 2 s lead only follows their noise (they move by 10 to 25 ms from one second to the next). Only an error that stays beyond 40 ms for 30 s without a break is corrected, at most once a minute, and every correction is in the log (`synced: ...`); a new D (the TV made a new connection, so a new backlog) or a changed `kitchen_offset_ms` moves the kitchen by the same amount at once (frames repeated or dropped, without a reading). No floor of the lag is learned in this mode, and the learned floor of the Lyrics mode is forgotten whenever the mode changes. `/kitchen/readings` is the last ten minutes of the Sonos's readings as CSV (position, the lag of each reading and its median, the error, the target, and how far the capture clock has moved against `System.nanoTime`), for a look afterwards; `/capture` counts the stray timestamps and the steps of that clock (`clock_outliers`, `clock_restarts`). The PCR runs on a clock that follows the capture clock only slowly (at most 10 ppm: on this box the capture clock holds the system clock to under 1 ppm for minutes) and not at all when its timestamps step by more than 8 ms, which they do: a period of the audio path or two, 46.8 ms once in a minute of a click test, while nothing is heard and the Sonos plays steadily. **The kitchen counts its lag from this clock** (a function of the number of the captured frame, the same that decides when the TV's stream writes it) and not from the timestamps of the capture, so such a step does not move one room against the other; the Lyrics mode, where the kitchen follows the living room's path, still counts from the timestamps. The TV's own crystal is another matter, and it gives no position to follow: `/tv/reanchor` (now) or `/tv/reanchor?at=boundary` (at the next change of song or two seconds of silence) restarts its stream, with the warm-up, and `tv_reanchor_min` does so by itself when the stream is that old.

**Keeping the box light.** The box is a Chromecast with 2 GB: the screensaver (`dreamx`), a streaming app that was left in the background and a handful of others took half of the four cores and all of the swap in a test, and the lyrics video and the capture are then late. So at each grab of the TV and every two minutes while the music plays (and there was sound in the last two minutes), every installed app except the ones the box stands on (this app, the two players whose audio is captured, Google Play services, the home screen, the system UI, the voice search, the remote service, the providers, input methods, Bluetooth, and what is in front) is asked to give up its background processes (`ActivityManager.killBackgroundProcesses`, the normal permission `KILL_BACKGROUND_PROCESSES`; the system kills only what is in the background and starts it again when it is wanted); the log says how many apps were asked and what the available memory and the free swap did ("cleanup (...): ..."). The setting `background_cleanup` (`true` by default) turns it off. An app that comes back after it was stopped (a usage event of it, an activity resumed or a foreground service started, since the last run) is asked again within 30 s and named in the log with the count (`comebacks` in the `cleanup` block of `/sys`); a foreground service is not a background process and `killBackgroundProcesses` does not end it, so the log then gives the command that does: `adb shell cmd appops set <package> RUN_ANY_IN_BACKGROUND ignore` (or `am force-stop <package>`). With the grant `WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS` the screensaver is switched off as well (no restore). `/sys` shows `load_avg`, `mem_available_mb`, `swap_free_mb`, the CPU of this app (`our_cpu_percent_of_one_core` and `_of_the_box`) and a `cleanup` block (when it ran last, what it asked, whether the grant is there, the screensaver).

**Synced must not freeze (stalls).** Two kinds of freeze were seen: the box held up (the connection thread did not read the ring for 0.9 s while the encoder needed 469 ms for a picture) and the TV's player pausing by itself (a write to it blocked 150 ms with the box idle). What the stream does now. *Video never holds up the sound:* the audio, the PCR and the padding go out on the slot clock whatever the video does; a picture is written when it is due, half a second after it was drawn (`video_budget_ms`: the encoder is given that, and the lyrics shown are those of the sound's own time, so the position stays exact, but a new cover and title come about 0.3 s after the sound); a picture whose last packet would leave later than the PCR lead less 150 ms after its due time is dropped together with the pictures that refer to it, up to the next key picture, which is asked for (`video_dropped`, `video_drop_episodes`, `video_sent_late` in `/tv/dlna` under `video`). *Threads:* the writer, the audio feeder, both encoders' output threads and the connection to the TV run at the priority of an audio thread (the first thread of each name logs what it got). *Song change:* the new track is blended over the old picture in a second (a cut changes every pixel at once, the most the encoder is ever asked), and the cover's colour and squares are made on their own thread. *What the log says:* every 5 s `conn N ... the data written was up to X ms old (it had waited up to Y ms in the ring when this thread took it)`, and as a WARNING with the state of the stream when X is over 300 ms; `WARNING the stream was held up` (the writer woke up late, the app was kept from running, pictures dropped, load and memory); `WARNING this app was not run by the system for N ms` from a watch that sleeps 20 ms at a time. A large Y with a small write time means the box did not run the thread; a small Y with a large write time means the TV did not read. *The TV's pauses:* a write that blocks `tv_pause_ms` (100 by default, 0 = off) or more once the burst is over, or a position (`GetPositionInfo`, every 5 to 10 s at a random moment, logged as `position probe:`, gated by `tv_position_probe` alone) that stands still while the clock goes on, marks the stream **suspect** (the kitchen is then about the pause ahead of the TV; shifting the kitchen later by it is only logged). With `tv_auto_reanchor` (off by default: the TV once timed out on the SetAVTransportURI of such a re-anchor and went to standby) the stream is made again at the next song boundary; without it the pause is counted and logged (`tv_pauses`, `suspect` in `/tv/dlna`) and `/tv/reanchor?at=boundary` does it by hand. This Philips does not implement the position (RelTime always 0:00:00, AbsTime NOT_IMPLEMENTED, the counts 2147483647): the probe sees that after three readings, logs it once, and never reports a standstill for such a TV. *tv_quiet:* during Synced play no call is made to the TV (no 2 s transport poll, no JointSpace refresh) except at the grab and the release; only the end of its connection tells the app it left. *tv_audio_only* (off; do not switch it on until the TV's latency for it is modelled): while the screen is off (displays Listening and Ambilight) the TV is given a transport stream of the AAC audio alone (no video PID, the clock on the audio, a place to start every 100 ms, the same rate and PCR lead); changing it or the display remakes the stream. It was on by default for an hour on 28 Oct: the TV played that stream **4.48 s later** than the latency model says (its preroll seems to count the bytes it is given, as with the variable bitrate), so the kitchen was that far ahead. `python3 spikes/tvclicks.py` records the click track with the Mac's microphone and prints the kitchen's offset against the TV, the whole seconds from the tenth clicks; check the two rooms with it after any change of the TV's stream. *Re-anchor:* by default the TV is stopped, its transport waited for, and the new item set, a 701 or 705 being tried again every 1.2 s for about 15 s. With `tv_reanchor_running` (off by default: the TV once timed out on it and went to standby) the item goes to the transport that is running with no Stop, and only if the TV takes nothing in 15 s is it stopped once and asked again. *The idle AudioTrack:* while the TV plays our stream (or sleeps) and nothing of Whole Flat's own plays, the living room's idle track is released instead of being rebuilt every 30 s when the HDMI sink does not take its silence (17 stalls in 9 minutes, each an HDMI route change). *Blocks and ghosts in the picture* (the TV concealing the pictures of a scroll, then frozen until the next key picture) mean a P picture reached it with its reference missing. What is done against that: the output buffers of the encoder that carry the same time are one picture (a picture given in slices was cut, its later slices thrown away as "a second picture with that time"); no picture is ever left out for its time (it gets the next tick; `same_time_pictures`, `encoder_split_pieces` in `/tv/dlna` under `video` count both); pictures leave in the order they came whatever due times they were given; every picture the late-video gate drops is named in the log with its kind (`WARNING a P picture was dropped (...)`); and the setting `tv_scroll` = `instant` makes the lines stand in their new places at once with a 150 ms fade of the two colours that change, instead of a scroll of 0.35 s that moves all the text (far fewer motion bits; `smooth` is the default). *Silence:* how long the music has been silent is counted from the level of the captured chunks and survives a capture that is made again (the box's audio route changes when the TV goes to standby, and the count that went with the capture said "never heard anything", which announced 30 minutes of silence seconds after music had played). *Memory:* the ring of the stream holds 6 s (6.8 MB at 9 Mbit/s, it was 12 s and 16 MB), the cover cache the last 3 covers (0.8 MB of squares each besides the picture itself), and `/sys` shows `rss_mb`, `rss_swapped_mb` and `native_heap_mb` of this app. *Swap:* every 5 s `/proc/vmstat` (`pswpin`, `pswpout`) is read and a WARNING `swap burst: N pages/s out` is logged above 300 pages a second, to put beside the other warnings (`swap` in `/sys`). *Apps that must not run while the music plays:* the cleaner asks every third-party app but Spotify and mediashell to stop at each grab and every two minutes; with `adb shell pm grant io.github.matijasalej.wholeflat android.permission.DUMP` it names those that still run, with their RSS (`dumpsys meminfo`; `still_running` in the `cleanup` block of `/sys`), and `third_party_apps` in `/sys` shows each one's RUN_ANY_IN_BACKGROUND and RUN_IN_BACKGROUND mode (`adb shell cmd appops set <package> RUN_ANY_IN_BACKGROUND ignore` plus `am force-stop <package>` is what keeps them stopped). *Wake-on-LAN:* a TV in standby that refuses `powerstate On` (or is not on after 3 s) gets the magic packet three times to the broadcast address, the subnet's and the TV (`tv_mac`, the wifi0 address; the one JointSpace reports is learned when the TV is on and used if the setting is empty).

**Calibrating the two rooms.** `curl 'http://<box-ip>:8080/tv/clicks?seconds=60'` puts a click track in place of the music in both streams (Synced mode must be live, and something must play in the Cast player for the kitchen to stream): one 5 ms click a second at the same capture frame in both, the TV's at 2 kHz (4 kHz every tenth), the kitchen's at 3 kHz (6 kHz); `/tv/clicks/stop` ends it. Measure the two from the doorway with the Mac tools; `tv_extra_ms` moves the two rooms together against the capture, `kitchen_offset_ms` the kitchen against the TV (the fine part: a microphone beside the TV and your seat differ by about 20 ms).

**A hung player.** The TV's player can hang: it takes the item, says TRANSITIONING and never asks for the stream, then answers 705 to everything and jumps to its tuner; a standby did not clear it on 23 Oct, the plug did. At rest a hung player looks like a free one (NO_MEDIA_PRESENT, no URI, no actions), so a start first looks at the transport (`SwitchPreflight`): empty or stopped, the item is set with no Stop; holding something, one Stop; still TRANSITIONING 5 s later and 3 s after a Stop, the player is hung and is given nothing. Two starts in a row that end in TRANSITIONING without a GET are hung too. Then Synced is not tried again (each try left the TV on its tuner), the mode is Lyrics, and the phone page says *The TV's player is stuck: unplug the TV for a minute, then pick a setting on the phone page*; the setting says the player is free, and that start is on probation (one more hang and it is stuck again). The state outlives a restart of the app (`player` in `/tv/dlna`, `tv_player` in `/sys`). Standby and an unplugged TV look the same from the network (neither answers), which is why only you can say that it is free. Every start also stops the TV before our stream ends, and gives back (Stop, back to HDMI) what it set when it fails or is cancelled. On 28 Oct the player hung on fresh starts that came soon after it was closed, or soon after the TV had its power back, and on none that came long after or as a restart: so a fresh start waits (`tv_boot_wait_s`, `tv_close_wait_s`), and the player is not closed for a silence (`tv_hold_silence_min` 0). The waits are on the safe side until a test measures them; `/tv/journal` has them in every grab (`boot_left_s`, `close_left_s`).

**Back to HDMI.** Giving the TV back is Stop. It usually returns to its HDMI input by itself; if it does not within 1.5 s, the app shows an activity of its own for an instant (it needs `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW`, granted for the capture), and if that does not do it within 2.5 s more and the TV says it is in its media player, it presses the TV's Back key. Every step is in the log with the time since Stop and what the TV shows, and `hdmi_return` in `/tv/dlna` says where it is (and which worked); `adb shell cmd hdmi_control onetouchplay` does it from the outside.

## Free storage

The Google TV has little storage, and the Spotify app's own cache has filled it to 97% once, which made the installation of this app fail. ## Calibration

The sync keeps the Sonos in step with the *moment the living room's audio chip presents each sample*. What sits behind that point (the HDMI link, the TV's audio delay on its optical output, the amplifier, and the Sonos's own output) is different in every flat, so the two rooms need one offset, measured once.

**With a Mac, from the doorway** (microphone where both rooms can be heard): the tool in `spikes/` (see `spikes/README.md`) starts the test signal over HTTP, records, matches the clicks of the two rooms and can write the result into the offset (`tworoom --apply`). The test signal is also usable by hand:

```sh
curl 'http://<tv-ip>:8080/calibrate/start?seconds=30'   # a start event through the whole pipeline
curl  http://<tv-ip>:8080/calibrate/stop
curl  http://<tv-ip>:8080/settings                       # read the settings
curl -X POST -d '{"kitchen_offset_ms": 35}' http://<tv-ip>:8080/settings
```

The test signal is one 5 ms click per second (Hann-windowed sine, half of full scale) at frame k·44100 from the start, and nothing else. The living room plays only the left channel (2 kHz, every 10th click at 4 kHz), the kitchen only the right channel (3 kHz, every 10th at 6 kHz), so a microphone can tell the rooms apart and count the clicks.

**By ear:** press **Test** on the settings screen, stand in the doorway and move the **Kitchen offset** slider until the low click and the high click fall together as one. If you hear the kitchen after the living room, the offset is positive.

## Sources of music

Where the music comes from is a small interface (`app/.../source/AudioSource.kt` and `native/src/source.rs`): a source delivers PCM frames with their sample rate and reports start, pause, resume and flush (a seek or a skip), the track and the volume. librespot feeds the ring from inside the native core. A source on the Kotlin side gets the app's `SourceBridge` as its sink and listener (`PlaybackService.sourceBridge`) and goes through the same ring, start events and sync as librespot. The ring plays 44.1 kHz, so a source with another rate has to convert first.

The **capture source** (`app/.../capture/CaptureSource.kt`) is the second one. It implements `AudioSource`, but its audio does not go through the ring: it cannot be slowed down, so `CaptureFeed` keeps the last 8 s in a buffer of its own (numbered frames, with a clock from `AudioRecord.getTimestamp`), and the kitchen's stream reads from there. It is captured at 44.1 kHz, the rate of what the Cast receiver plays and of the ring, so that nothing is resampled and both modes send the Sonos the same kind of stream. The living room plays no captured audio (the Cast receiver plays it by itself). `SourceArbiter` decides which source feeds the speakers.

## The kitchen's stream and the Sonos

The Sonos pulls the stream from the app over HTTP (`http://<tv-ip>:8080/k/<token>.flac`, or `.wav`) after the app has set the URL with UPnP. It does that in its own way, and the app follows it:

- **Every connection is served for itself.** The Sonos opens a *probe* GET when the URL is set (it reads the headers and one frame and closes it by itself, within about 40 ms), the stream proper after Play, and a HEAD. Each GET is served from the first frame until the *Sonos* closes it or the session ends; the app never closes one because a newer one came or because Play was sent. A probe gets a second of audio at once and then real time, a stream after Play its lead of 1.5 s (a captured one, as it is captured), and the first write of a connection is the headers, the FLAC STREAMINFO (or the RIFF header) and every frame that is ready, in one write. A HEAD gets exactly the headers of a GET and changes nothing. Of the connections that come after Play the newest one that is still open is the one the sync steers, and the older takes over when it ends.
- **Format.** The settings `kitchen_format` (`auto`, the default, `flac` or `wav`) and `kitchen_flac_block` (4096, the default, 1152 or 576 frames per FLAC frame) set the stream (`curl 'http://<tv-ip>:8080/settings?kitchen_format=wav'`; the kitchen restarts with it). `auto` is WAV for captured music, FLAC for Spotify's. A FLAC frame can only be sent once all of its samples exist, so in a live stream the block size is added delay: 93 ms for 4096, 26 ms for 1152, 13 ms for 576 at 44.1 kHz. WAV has no blocks (it is sent in chunks of 10 ms) and starts sooner on the Sonos (0.19 s from Play to sound against 0.29 s for FLAC, and with a lead of 80 ms a lag of 320 to 350 ms where FLAC needed about 0.9 s of audio before it played). The delay from Play to sound is kept and learned per format. The WAV header claims a length of 0x7FFFF000 bytes (about 3.4 hours) and the response says the same in its Content-Length; the stream ends there, and the kitchen starts again.
- **Failed starts back off.** A start fails when preparing the speaker or Play fails, or the session is re-joined before it went well (15 s of readings that say PLAYING). The first failed start is tried again soon; after the second the next one waits 10 s, then 30 s, 60 s, 120 s and at most 5 minutes (for captured streams as well as Spotify's), so that a speaker that cannot take the stream is not started and cut off over and over. A session that goes well starts it over. The log says why, in the speaker's words as far as it has them: its transport state and status (asked afresh), the last state of its websocket and any `playbackError` (the app subscribes to the group's playback events). `kitchen.failed_starts` and `backoff_s` in the status tell where it is; a re-join asked for on the debug page or by a changed setting does not count.
- **Who has the Sonos.** Only something else that is *playing* on the speaker (PLAYING, TRANSITIONING) keeps the kitchen out. Something paused or stopped is free, and so is a paused stream of ours from an earlier run, unless someone paused it on the speaker itself during the session that just ended (then the kitchen stays quiet until the speaker is stopped or plays, but **not after a power cycle**: the power button pauses first and the speaker then goes off; when it comes back it is wanted again, and the kitchen joins by itself). Before the stream is set the app asks what the speaker holds right then: if it plays something else, the start is dropped (not a failure); a **paused foreign session, a Spotify Connect one (`x-sonos-vli:...`) for instance, ignores a UPnP Stop and would stay on the transport, so the speaker is switched to its own queue first** (`SetAVTransportURI x-rincon-queue:<RINCON>#0` and Stop) and the stream is set after that.
- **Evidence.** `GET /kitchen/last-sessions` keeps the connections and the speaker's states of the last 10 sessions (see the API table), which is what a device test is read from.

## How the sync works

- The living room is the clock master and is never adjusted after a start. Every discontinuity of the music (seek, skip, resume, recovery) is a *start event*: the Rust core drops what has not been played, and both rooms start again at the same frame.
- At a start the app prepares the Sonos (mute, stop, set the stream URL), then places the first frame in the living room's audio track so that it is heard when the Sonos's first sound arrives (about 0.29 s after Play), using `AudioTrack.getTimestamp`, and sends Play at that moment.
- During playback the app asks the Sonos for its play position five times a second over its local websocket, maps it to the music frame it is playing, and compares that with when the living room plays the same frame. Small errors are removed by dropping or repeating single frames in the stream (never more than one per 10 ms); an error of more than 40 ms for three readings restarts only the Sonos stream at the right frame.
- If the Sonos goes away (sleep, Bluetooth, another app), the living room keeps playing. The app polls the Sonos every 3 s and listens for its announcements; when it is free again, it joins the music in progress.
- In capture mode there is no living room to follow, so the stream starts a little in the past (`capture_lead_ms`, counted back from the newest captured frame at the moment Play goes out), the first readings of the Sonos's position measure how long after the capture the kitchen sounds, and that lag is then brought to `kitchen_target_ms` at 3 ms per second at most and held there with the same frame trims (a tolerance of 5 ms), never below the floor (see "If Spotify refuses Whole Flat"). If the lag itself steps (the Sonos stalled and filled its buffer again), it is measured again instead of restarting the stream (`kitchen.capture_relocks`); the stream is restarted for a lag beyond 5 s, after six such steps, or at a sign that the Sonos runs dry at the lag it was brought to.

## Building

The APK is built by CI only (GitHub Actions, `.github/workflows/build.yml`); no Android SDK, JDK or NDK is needed locally. Every push builds the release APK and uploads it as the `WholeFlatAudio` artifact of the run. A push to `main` also publishes a GitHub release `build-<run number>`, which is what the Downloader URL above serves. The workflow also runs the Rust unit tests (`cargo test --lib` in `native/`) and the JVM unit tests (`gradle :app:testDebugUnitTest`: the sync math, the controller, the FLAC encoder, the settings).

`native/Cargo.lock` is committed so that CI builds the dependency versions that were checked. The Rust core also compiles, and its unit tests run, on a plain host toolchain; only the Android build itself needs the NDK.

The repository needs three secrets:

| Secret | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_B64` | the PKCS12 keystore, base64 encoded |
| `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD` | its password (also used for the key) |
| `ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS` | the key alias |

The keystore lives at `~/.config/flat_audio/release.p12`. **Back it up**: a build signed with another key cannot be installed over this one, you would have to uninstall first and lose the cached login. Without the secrets the release is signed with the debug key so that test builds still work.

Layout: `native/` is the Rust core (librespot behind JNI, built by `cargo-ndk`; `ring.rs` is the frame ring with the epochs, `calib.rs` the test signal, `keyguard.rs` the back-off for refused audio keys, `source.rs` the way in for other sources), `app/` the Kotlin app (`sync/` is the plain-Kotlin math that the JVM tests cover, and so is most of `capture/`: the activity detector, the arbiter, the buffer and clock, the lag lock, the volume mirror; the Android parts of `capture/` are the source, the consent activity, the service and the media session watcher; `LivingRoom` the audio output; `Kitchen`, `SonosSoap`, `SonosWs`, `SonosFinder` and `KitchenStream` the kitchen; `TvControl` the TV's JointSpace; Synced mode is `TvRenderer` (the TV's DLNA player), `SyncedController` (the mode manager, the kitchen's timing, the screen and lights), `TvTsPipeline` with `TvVideoEncoder`, `TvAudioEncoder`, `SceneDrawer` and `LyricsRenderer` (the live video), and in `sync/` the plain-Kotlin parts that the JVM tests cover: `TsMuxer` (the constant-rate transport stream), `TsWriter`, `TsHistory`, `Warmup`, `ModeManager`, `Display`, `SyncedTiming`, `Lrc`, `LrcLib`, `LyricsRepository`, `LyricsLayout`, `ClickTrack`). `spikes/` holds the measurement tooling that the design is based on. The app targets SDK 34 on purpose: from SDK 35 on Android no longer lets a `BOOT_COMPLETED` receiver start a media playback foreground service.

librespot is used at a pinned git revision (the crates.io 0.8.0 release does not build). Two crates are vendored in `native/vendor/`, each header of `Cargo.toml` lists the changes. `librespot-core` has three small changes (client token, user agent and claimed version present the desktop identity: the receiver pins the desktop client id, which desktop Spotify controllers need, and upstream would still announce Android), plus the spacing of audio key requests after an error and a tap on the commands the Spotify app sends. `librespot-playback` is librespot PR #1799 (a refused audio key no longer makes Spirc skip through the queue) with one change of ours: the player stays in its loading state, so that the same track can be loaded again later.
